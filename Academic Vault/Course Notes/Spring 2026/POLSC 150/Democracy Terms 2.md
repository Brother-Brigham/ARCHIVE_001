**Terms**:

1. Separation of Powers/Checks and Balances: power is shared/divided between government branches so no branch can acquire too much power

2. Executive: Enforce law
    1. Head of State: The top representative of a state _to other_ states. 

    2. Head of Government: The top policy-maker _within_ a country

    3. President: Head of state, can also be head of government. Usually directly elected by the public

    4. Semi-Presidential: a state has a president and a prime-minister

3. Executive AND Legislative: where members of the executive are, _first and foremost,_ members of the legislative
    1. Prime Minister: usually leader of the largest party in the legislature, indirectly elected. Head of government, often functions as head of state. 

    2. Coalition government: No party gains a majority in the legislature, so two or more parties will cooperate to create a "majority". 

4. Legislative: Create law
    1. Parliament. most countries have a parliament where the US has Congress.

    2. Unicameral legislature. Only one legislative, like if the US had only the House of Representatives

	3. Bicameral legislature. Two legislative bodies, lower and upper, like the US House and Senate.

5. Judiciary: Interpret law
    1. Constitutional Court: The courts who decide if laws are constitutional

	2. Criminal Court: The courts who decide if someone broke the law
    
	3. Civil Court: The courts who decide if someone didn't necessarily break the law, but if someone's behavior inhibited someone else's ability to live in a manner to which they are accustomed.
