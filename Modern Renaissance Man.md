# Polymath Study Plan: Analyst-Style Systematic Theology
## Goals

- **Long-term:** Develop a systematic theology within the Latter-day Saint tradition (confessional), using an intelligence-analyst approach rather than a typical academic one.

- **Broader aim:** Become a modern-day polymath: deep in theology and source languages, fluent in logic, statistics, programming, and select sciences, and able to move ideas between fields.

- **Starting position:** Strong in writing, analysis, and primary source research. Weak in math, statistics, the sciences, and programming. Language background: 1 year Biblical Hebrew, 1.5 years Classical Greek.

- **Time budget:** About 8-10 hours per week.

### Method commitments

- Name confessional commitments up front as assumptions.

- Distinguish evidence, inference, and assumption.

- State confidence levels explicitly.

- Red-team my own conclusions.

- Be explicit about where I am analyzing and where I am committing.

### Open decisions

- [ ] How does revelation function in the system: a source weighed like others, or a foundational assumption?

- [ ] Descriptive of current LDS teaching, or constructive (proposing coherent positions where the tradition is underdeveloped)?

# The 4 Pillars of a Modern Polymath

To design a well-rounded curriculum, it helps to structure your learning across four distinct domains:

### 1. The Mind (Analytical & Scientific Literacy)

 Focus: Fundamental physics or biology, data literacy, coding/algorithmic thinking, and formal logic.

 The Goal: Understanding how the physical, natural, and digital worlds actually work so you can navigate complex systems.

### 2. The Expression (Arts & Humanities)

 Focus: Philosophy (ethics, epistemology), world history, narrative writing, music, or visual arts.

 The Goal: Cultivating aesthetic taste, emotional depth, historical perspective, and the ability to communicate ideas persuasively.

### 3. The Vessel (Physical Mastery & Practical Craft)

 Focus: Functional strength/mobility, a movement discipline (martial arts, climbing, dance), and a hands-on craft (cooking, woodworking, basic mechanics).

 The Goal: Embodying your intelligence. A polymath isn't just an armchair thinker—they interact dynamically with the physical world.

### 4. The Agent (Social & Strategic Wisdom)

 Focus: Rhetoric, negotiation, financial strategy, and psychological self-regulation.

 The Goal: Navigating human systems and turning internal knowledge into real-world impact.

---

The Blueprint: Aim for an "M-Shaped" Profile

Pure generalists risk knowing a tiny bit about everything without bringing real value to anything. A modern polymath builds deep, functional expertise in 2 to 3 core domains while maintaining a strong general baseline across the rest.

---

Instead of choosing between deep spikes and broad domains, structure your curriculum around 3- to 6-month cycles:

1. Maintain a Baseline Across All 4 Domains (30% Energy)

Set small, non-negotiable, routine habits for every domain so none of them go completely dark.

 Mind: Read 1 article or book chapter on science/logic.

 Expression: Read a few pages of history or write a weekly journal entry.

 Vessel: Walk 8,000 steps and do 15 minutes of mobility work daily.

 Agent: Keep your personal finances strictly tracked.

2. Pick ONE "Deep Spike" Per Season (70% Energy)

Pour the bulk of your active learning energy into one specific skill or subject within one domain until you hit a threshold of functional competence.

An Example 1-Year Cycle:

* Q1 & Q2 (The Mind/Vessel Spike): Intensive coding bootcamp + learning basic weightlifting mechanics.
* Q3 (The Expression Spike): Deep-dive into 20th-century geopolitical history and conversational Spanish.
* Q4 (The Agent Spike): Focused study on public speaking and negotiation.
